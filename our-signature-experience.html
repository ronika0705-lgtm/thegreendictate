Our Signature Experience: The 2029 Table

By The Dictators · Hospitality Pro League 2026–27 · Sustainable Hospitality

Most tasting menus tell you about the chef. The 2029 Table tells you about the supply chain, and it turns out to be the better story.

Five courses, five decrees

The 2029 Table is a shared five-course experience, indicatively priced at INR 1,450 per guest (final pricing to be confirmed before launch). Every course arrives with a Decree Card. The card shows where the ingredient came from, its Radius, how the kitchen transformed it, and the waste-reduction decision behind it.

You don’t just eat the story. You see the operating logic.

What a cauliflower leaf can become

In most kitchens, cauliflower leaves and broccoli stems are trimmed and binned. At The Green Dictate they are folded into a besan crust and fried into Stalk & Leaf Kebabs. Other transformations on the table include:

  • Approved bakery surplus, such as day-old kulcha, becomes a fresh dish served with whey dal.
  • Bread and jaggery become a zero-waste halwa with citrus-peel candy.
  • Citrus peels, tulsi and mint become a herbal cordial and a Peel Spritz.
  • Whey from paneer-making becomes chaach, chaas raita, whey-marinated tikka and chhena poda.
Same city. Same system. Two ends of one problem, now on one plate.

The drink that follows the day’s harvest

The Table pairs naturally with our signature beverage programme, The Impound: a bar with no fixed menu. Each day the kitchen and the mandi hand the bar a short list of ingredients at their best, such as citrus peels, whey, ripe fruit, herb stems, bread and tea leaves. The bartenders build the day’s drinks from that list, and a chalkboard shows the five hero ingredients with their weight and source.

Zero-proof drinks use the same hero ingredients and get equal billing. Pre-batched cocktails come in returnable Evidence Bottles, and guests who return them earn Tribute credit. A shelf of date-stamped ferments, The Holding Cell, doubles as décor. We are upfront that spirits cannot be sourced from surplus: the savings come from mixers, garnishes, infusions and packaging.

Why it works

The technique behind it is cooking for maximum usable yield, preserving surplus through pickling, fermenting and drying, and separating trim by destination so that nothing is wasted by accident. The cultural connection is a very Indian one: the thrift of the home kitchen, the logic of the mandi, whey and jaggery and kachri, all treated with fine-dining respect.

And because it runs on real records, the evening ends with a Compliance Receipt showing what your table helped rescue.
