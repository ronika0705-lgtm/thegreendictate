The Story Behind Our Brand

By The Dictators · Hospitality Pro League 2026–27 · Sustainable Hospitality

It was a Saturday banquet, 11:40 pm. The guests had gone home happy. In the back, untouched rice, vegetables and bread were being tipped into bins because “that was standard procedure.”

The next morning, at the mandi, perfectly good produce was being marked down because it had missed its slot. Same city. Same system. Two ends of one problem. That contrast is where The Green Dictate began.

The problem with “green”

Sustainable dining has a trust problem. Guests are asked to believe a policy page, a logo or a note at the bottom of the menu, and they have no way to check any of it. Meanwhile restaurants over-buy, over-produce and over-portion, and suppliers lose value on food that is perfectly edible.

We asked what hospitality will look like in 2029, and our answer was that guests will stop accepting sustainability as décor. They will want proof.

The concept: a government office you actually want to visit

The Green Dictate is a 50-seat speakeasy restaurant in Delhi NCR. From the street it looks like a dull government office: frosted glass, a token machine, a clerk and a notice board. You take a token, receive a Citizen Card, and the notice board opens into The Chamber, a dim room of brass, velvet and candlelight.

The inspiration is mafia-era cinema and strict bureaucracy, played for laughs and with real discipline underneath. We call ourselves a dictatorship, a benevolent one, and we speak in the language of rules: Obey the menu. Respect the harvest. The humour is the doorway. The discipline is real.

The theatre is the doorway. The real innovation is the kitchen and the operating system behind it.

The theme: Sustainable Hospitality

Our assigned Future of Hospitality theme is Sustainable Hospitality: local, circular and resource conscious. We turned each word into a house rule.

  • Local. We source nearby, employ nearby and tell local stories. Every dish carries a Radius showing how far its ingredients travelled.
  • Circular. We cook surplus first, use stem-to-root and nose-to-tail cooking, and preserve through pickling, fermenting and drying.
  • Resource conscious. We measure food waste, water, energy, packaging and occupancy, then use the data to change tomorrow’s purchasing and production.
  • Rule Four. Each month one guest-proposed rule becomes law.

The experience we want to create

We want an evening that feels discovered, not templated. You arrive, take a token, and slip into a hidden room. Your plate tells you where it came from. A Countdown Wall shows what the restaurant has actually saved. When you leave, a Compliance Receipt records the kilos rescued, the local spend and the distance your food travelled.

We are also honest about the limits. We don’t claim every ingredient will be surplus, or that waste can literally become zero. We design purchasing, cooking, preservation, service and recovery so that avoidable waste has fewer places to hide.

AI helps behind the scenes by forecasting covers, reading supplier photos and flagging anomalies. But AI recommends and people decide: food safety, sourcing, the menu and hospitality remain human.

By 2029, the bin is the most embarrassing room in any restaurant.
